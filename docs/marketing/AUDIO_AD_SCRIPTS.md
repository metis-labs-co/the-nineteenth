# The Nineteenth — Audio Ad Scripts (Spotify / Podcast)

All-speech, host-read scripts for Spotify Ad Studio and podcast placements. No music bed — host-read ads work *because* they don't sound like ads.

> Brand voice, positioning and hashtags: see `README.md`. Channel/spend plan: see `SOCIAL_STRATEGY.md`.
> Market: Australia first. Australian English, casual ("comp", "mate", "sorted"), no US spellings.

**Read-pace reference:** ~2.5 words/sec conversational. 15s ≈ 40 words · 30s ≈ 75–80 · 60s ≈ 150–165. Beats eat ~0.5s each.

---

## 15-second pre-roll

> **[Brisk, casual]**
>
> Quick one — if you're the mate who runs the golf comp, ditch the spreadsheet. The Nineteenth sets up a comp in minutes, scores your whole group from one phone, and works offline. Free to start — just search **"The Nineteenth"** wherever you get your apps.

**~44 words · ≈15–16 sec.** One hook, one CTA. No room for proof — name + benefit + action only.

## 60-second (host-read podcast length)

> **[Conversational, unhurried — this is a recommendation, not a read]**
>
> Alright, quick one — if you organise golf with your mates, this one's for you.
>
> You know how it goes. Someone's gotta run the comp: who's playing, who's paired with who, who's keeping score — then chasing everyone for their numbers after. It ends up a spreadsheet, a chaotic group chat, and one very stressed mate. **[beat]**
>
> There's a better way. It's an app called **The Nineteenth.** You set up a competition in about four steps — it sorts the pairings, rotates partners so it's fair, and does all the handicap maths for you. On the day, one person scores the whole group from a single phone. No fumbling between shots. And the bit I love: it works completely offline, so when you lose signal out on the course, it just keeps going and syncs back at the clubhouse. Live leaderboard the whole way round.
>
> Free to start — just search **"The Nineteenth"** wherever you get your apps. You'll have your next comp sorted before your next tee time.

**~164 words · ≈60–62 sec.** Full arc: problem → solution → the "bit I love" (offline) → CTA. The personal aside ("the bit I love") is what makes host-read convert.

## Swap-in options (any length)

**Alternate openers:**
- *Relatable fail:* "Quick one — ever turned up to a golf day and nobody knew the pairings? Yeah. Been there."
- *Direct:* "Quick one for the golfers — organising a comp shouldn't need a spreadsheet wizard."

**Alternate CTAs:**
- *Platform-agnostic (default):* "Search 'The Nineteenth' wherever you get your apps." — covers iOS + Android (Spotify's audience is split)

- *With landing page:* "Search 'The Nineteenth', or head to [URL]." — **confirm the real domain first** (`marketing/CLAUDE.md` lists `theninetenth.app`, missing an "i")
- *With promo code:* "Use code POD at sign-up." — only if a code/offer actually exists in-app

## Delivery & production notes

- **No music.** Dry, natural read. The whole value of host-read is that it doesn't sound produced.
- Say **"The Nineteenth"** clearly — the app name is the only searchable asset, so it must land. Twice across a 30s/60s read is fine.
- **Don't read the handle** `@the.nineteenth.golf` aloud — dots are a mouthful and easy to mishear. App name only.
- Land the closing line ("sorted before your next tee time") with a slight smile — it's the button.
- For programmatic/announcer reads, keep the same words but tighten beats to hit the slot exactly.

## Spotify Ad Studio practical notes

- Ad Studio standard slots are **15s and 30s**; the 60s is for host-read podcast deals negotiated directly with shows.
- Ad Studio audio runs with a **clickable companion banner** — so the verbal CTA can be lighter (the tap does the work). For host-read podcast spots there's no banner, so the verbal CTA must carry it fully (these scripts already do).
- **Targeting (AU):** age 25–55, interests Golf + Sports; consider podcast-genre targeting (Sports, Comedy, News) over music for higher intent.
- **Creative testing:** run the 15s and 30s against each other on the same audience for 2 weeks before scaling spend. Judge on installs-per-dollar, not impressions.
- Tie spend to the **kill/scale thresholds** in `SOCIAL_STRATEGY.md` §6.
